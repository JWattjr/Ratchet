# Surface brief: Release overview

## Mode

Operate.

## Audience and task

A release owner or reviewer needs to decide whether a candidate upgrade changed only what its declaration allowed. A reviewer should understand the product within seconds and be able to follow the evidence without a wallet.

## Direction contract

**Ratchet Calibration Bench**: a cheerful precision workshop translated into a release inspection surface. The product behaves like an engineered instrument, not a generic dashboard. One modest release companion marks the selected gate; data, diffs, and proof remain visually dominant.

- **World:** A cool white and sky-blue calibration surface with mint, amber, periwinkle, and coral enamel markers. Fine registration lines, softly rounded instruments, and translucent overlays create a tactile but precise bench.
- **First viewport:** Ratchet identity and the short promise sit beside a real Studio Next availability indicator. The selected release shows its live contract state, current and candidate implementation hashes, a paired declaration/observation strip, one clear “Inspect evidence” action, and its current verdict gate. No marketing hero precedes the working release.
- **Visitor path:** Declaration → replay observations → matching changes → validator interpretation → deterministic contract action. A reviewer can jump to source receipts, corpus coverage, invariants, transaction lifecycle, bond receipt, and explorer proof.
- **Signature interaction:** A small inspection shuttle moves through five numbered stops on the release flight recorder. Each stop progressively reveals declaration, replay, comparison, validator result, and contract consequence. Selecting a stop also works without motion; reduced-motion preference replaces travel with a short cross-fade or immediate state change.
- **Cross-surface reach:** The calibration bench grammar supports the declaration dossier, responsive evidence sequence, verdict station, bond receipt, and methodology pages. It remains legible for dense JSON-derived evidence and small-screen serial reading.
- **Honest risk:** A workshop can become cute decoration. The release companion is one small state cue only; status is always named and shaped, and exact hashes and evidence remain easy to inspect.
- **Typography:** Use a friendly, engineered sans for labels and prose, with a narrow numeric face only for hashes, IDs, and transaction metadata. Avoid large editorial serifs, default Inter/Space Grotesk, and decorative monospace text.
- **Responsive rules:** At desktop and laptop widths, show aligned declared and observed columns with a shared baseline. At 768px and 390px, switch the comparison to a declared → observed → decision sequence; never scale a wide table down. Keep tap targets at least 44px.
- **Motion rules:** One short positional reveal communicates progress. No flashing, auto-playing loops, animated failure states, or information available only while moving.

## Build path

Code-led, pinned by the request. No approved comp was selected or generated.
