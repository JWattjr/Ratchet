# Ratchet direction contract

## Chosen world

**Ratchet Calibration Bench** combines precision-instrument calibration benches with a friendly release inspection workflow. It uses a clear pale-blue work surface, a few enamel-like state markers, registered comparison lines, and one restrained release shuttle.

## Grounded candidate spread

1. **The Release Flight Recorder** — one continuous strip naturally carries the product’s required chronology and is the grounded top candidate.
2. **Transit Signal Desk** — a signal board gives the three gates a clear operational language.
3. **Maintenance Logbook** — a compact service record keeps declarations, attempts, and proof in a durable sequence.
4. **Ratchet Calibration Bench** — a precision instrument provides a natural place to align a frozen declaration with observed evidence.
5. **Field Engineer Sticker System** — short labels and inspection marks make exact IDs quick to scan.
6. **Mechanical Construction Kit** — modular pieces make declared storage and capability changes concrete.
7. **Colorful Quality-Control Station** — repeated checks and pass/hold/reject gates make the decision process legible.

The assigned direction is candidate four. The product name and required flight-recorder metaphor remain visible in the direction without taking over its entire visual identity.

## Form

One calibration bench holds the full release review. The upper rail establishes Studio Next and the selected declaration; the main specimen aligns the frozen declaration, replay observation, and validator decision; a compact side instrument summarizes release counts. The flight recorder is an evidence-navigation strip within that bench. On narrow screens, the side instrument and comparison stages stack beneath the evidence they summarize.

## Challenger weighing

Each challenger was fused with Ratchet’s product facts before comparison on audience identification and product clarity.

- **Reversible origami fold sequence — competitive.** It is less recognizable to release operators than a calibration bench, but its reversible reveal maps clearly to reviewing one evidence stage at a time. Keep it as a full alternate.
- **Cloud quarry — declined.** Release operators do not identify with a surreal quarry, and cloud blocks make precise provenance and evidence gaps less clear. Raise its visible cut-line discipline: a missing artifact gets a bounded, labeled slot.
- **Phosphor crisis wall — declined.** A crisis room is a weak audience cue and its alarms compete with the evidence. Raise its reserved-severity discipline: strong status color appears only in a restrained rail, paired with a label and shape, with no flashing.
- **Registered comparison folio — competitive.** The specimen world is a weaker operator cue, while its fixed-scale overlays substantially improve product clarity. Keep it as a full alternate, translated to cool paper and engineered sans type.
- **Broadcast teletext — declined.** Operators can benefit from stable addresses, but a strict retro grid is not a credible release-review surface and constrains evidence legibility. Raise its stable-index discipline: release, attempt, and transaction IDs remain short and keyboard-addressable.
- **Pixel arcade — declined.** The game world does not improve operator recognition or evidence clarity and risks making rollback feel playful. Raise its discrete-state discipline: use a small set of status shapes that remain clear with motion disabled.

These are craft donations, not visual motifs. One calibration-bench world owns the shipped interface.

## First viewport contract

- Prove the working product immediately: “Upgrades must stay inside what they declare.”
- Show live Studio Next connection status and configured deployed contract address. An unavailable RPC or missing deployment is labeled honestly.
- Select one seeded release only when it comes from a successful contract read; never label a local fixture as chain state.
- Keep both implementation hashes and the declaration-to-replay comparison in view.
- Make the active gate obvious, with the exact verdict code and a human summary.
- Provide one primary evidence-inspection action.
- Use the small release shuttle as a state cue; the evidence and verdict take visual priority.

## Design system commitments

- Light, cool work surface; deep ink for technical text; sky/periwinkle for system state, mint for ADVANCE, amber for HOLD, coral for ROLLBACK.
- Every status has text and a shape as well as color.
- Friendly engineered sans labels; numeric face limited to IDs, hashes, and block references.
- Fine rules, aligned plates, and tactile markers support comparison and scanning.
- No black neon dashboard, glass, gradient decoration, generic dashboard card grid, large editorial serif, fake terminal, or alarm animation.
- Body copy is direct, kind, and precise. Serious rollback language stays calm.
